## Departures from standard practice

Ranked by how much they can change a conclusion.

### 1. No gap between training and validation windows (affects early stopping, not the test)

Each session-1 trial is split 80 % training / 20 % validation with no gap. Windows are built over the whole trial, so the
first validation windows take their **inputs** from the end of the training part. The targets differ and the session-2
test is unaffected, but validation is partly made of data the model trained on, so early stopping (and the learning-rate
schedule) watch a slightly optimistic validation loss. The share grows with window length:

| Window | Validation windows whose inputs start in the training part |
| --- | --- |
| 200 ms | 1.1 % |
| 500 ms | 2.6 % |
| 1 s | 5.5 % |
| 2 s | 11.3 % |
| 5 s | 29.8 % |
| 10 s | 63.7 % |

Standard practice for time series ("purged" splits) leaves a gap of one window span between training and validation.
**Fix:** start validation windows one window span after the training cut (`ML/research/splits.py::build` and
`streams`). Negligible for 200 ms; it matters for 2 s and longer.

### 2. Two design choices were made on test scores

Batch 64 and the 2nd-order envelope were chosen by comparing session-2 test scores. Standard practice is to choose on
validation and report the test once. Checked afterwards on session-1 validation loss (same seeds):
- **2nd-order low-pass:** validation agrees, lower loss for 6/6 people (median ratio 0.66). The choice stands.
- **Batch 64:** validation **disagrees**, higher loss for 4/6 people (median ratio 1.12). Batch 64 was chosen for run
  time (about 5x faster), not accuracy, and should be reported that way.

### 3. EMG scale includes the validation part

The session-1 maximum per channel (`trainmax`) is taken over the whole session-1 trials, training and validation parts.
Standard practice fits every scaler on the training split only. This only affects validation (session 2 is never used);
it is the original pipeline's convention.

### 4. Statistics

Settings are compared by per-person medians over 5 or 10 seeds, with the seed range. There are no confidence intervals
or tests. With 6 people, a paired test across people (sign test or Wilcoxon on per-person means) is the right next
step; 6/6 people in the same direction gives p ≈ 0.03 (two-sided).

### 5. Smaller points

- **Overlap and training amount are confounded:** fewer windows means fewer updates per epoch under the same epoch limit
  (by choice; stated in each overlap setting's description).
- **Inputs are not standardized:** EMG ÷ maximum (0–1) and angle in radians (about −0.8 to 0.25) are already of order 1;
  z-scoring is the usual default but should change little.
- **Runs are not bit-reproducible:** seeds are fixed but TensorFlow's deterministic ops are not enabled; library versions
  are recorded in every run.
- **The target is smoothed with a zero-phase FIR when down-sampling** (about ±0.1 s). That is a label, not an input, and is
  standard for offline targets.
- **Hyperparameters** (LSTM 8x2, dropout 0.3, learning rate) come from the original model and Truong et al.; they were
  not tuned on this data (also raised in the mid-project review).

## Follow-up (same day)

- **Purged split added** (`"val_gap": true` in every 2nd-order window-study setting). The 2nd-order reference runs made
  before it are kept as `W00n_reference_nogap`.
- **IES had no passive torque subtracted anywhere.** IES recorded no relaxed holds, and the old automatic rule finds no
  passive trials either, so its "active torque" target was total torque in A0 and every earlier experiment (the docs said
  it "used the old rule"; corrected). The 2nd-order window study now sets `"metadata_passive": true`, which subtracts the
  passive torques noted at each position during IES's MVC trials (`METADATA_PASSIVE` in `ML/config.py`). Their sign and
  size match IES's measured torque at rest at every position.
- **Session-2 passive torque for the test** confirmed per person: JM, EG, CH and HM use their own session-2 relaxed
  holds; YES uses the hand-entered session-2 worksheet values.
- **Per-angle target scaling (`balance: scale`) is not leakage.** Each angle's scale is the spread of the training-part
  torque in session 1. At test time the factor is picked from the trial's mean angle; picking it from the angle input at
  each predicted moment instead (what a real-time system would do) gives the same factor for all 421,968 test samples of
  the six people: the angle wobbles at most 0.009 rad within a trial while positions are at least 0.10 rad apart.
- **Per-angle loss scaling removed** from the current round (`"balance": "none"`, plain mean-squared error): neither
  Zhang 2022 nor Truong 2023 balances by condition, and in the October study it traded better plantarflexed scores for up to
  36 % worse error at other angles. The 32 reference runs made with it are kept as `W00s_reference_scaled`.
