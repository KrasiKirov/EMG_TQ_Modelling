3

The final model, with every design choice. Data: six people, trained on day 1, tested on day 2. Inputs: the four muscle envelopes, band-passed 30 to 300 Hz, rectified, low-passed at 2 Hz and sampled at 100 Hz, plus the ankle angle, in 0.2-second windows. Each muscle is divided by its day-1 maximum; there is no auto-gain. The target is total torque, so no passive table is needed. Positions are balanced: each angle’s target is divided by its swing during training, so every angle counts the same. The model is an LSTM with two layers of 8 units and 30% dropout, trained with Nadam at a learning rate of 0.003 and early stopping. On day 2, a 10-second calibration per angle fits a stretch and a shift. Results are medians over 3 seeds, then over angles, scored from 30 seconds to the end of each trial. With the calibration, the toes-down error falls from 0.86 to 0.35 Nm against a typical toes-down peak of about 6 Nm. VAF hardly moves with the calibration, because VAF ignores offsets, and most of the toes-down error was an offset; that is why we report R² and MAE next to VAF. A0, which predicts active torque, is the best model without a calibration, and with the calibration it ends up about the same as N0. We keep N0 + calibration because it needs no passive table and it improved every person.
