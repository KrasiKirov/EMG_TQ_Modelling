2. A 10-second calibration

What we tried

Correct the drift between session 1 and session 2. The EMG and the resting torque change a bit each session, and differently for each person.

How

Record the first 10 s of each angle in session 2 with the torque sensor and fit a simple line to correct the prediction. The LSTM is not retrained. (Next slide.)

Result

With balanced positions, toes-down error about 50% lower than last semester (0.85 → 0.43 Nm). Better for 5 of 6 people; HM gets slightly worse.

Needs the torque sensor for 10 s per angle · tried after the main study

The remaining error was mostly drift between sessions: the EMG reads a bit bigger or smaller, and the resting torque moves. It is different for each person, so instead of guessing it we measure it. The first 10 seconds of each angle in session 2 are recorded with the torque sensor and used to fit a correction line. The model itself is not retrained. Those 10 seconds are never scored. Together with balanced positions, the toes-down error goes from 0.85 to 0.43 Nm, about half of last semester. Other angles are about a third lower. Five of six people improve; HM gets slightly worse. Limits: needs the torque sensor for 10 seconds, and it was tried after the main study.
