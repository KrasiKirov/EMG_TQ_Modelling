What the two changes are meant to achieve, with numbers measured on the recordings themselves rather than on model results. First, auto-gain keeps the EMG size more consistent between sessions: at the toes-down angles, the share of muscle-and-angle cases where session 2’s EMG size per newton-metre is off by more than 25% drops from 53% to 30%, across the five people it applies to; over all angles it goes from 32% to 18%. It does not fix every case, and a few get worse. Second, balancing makes the toes-down angles count: for YES their share of the training loss goes from about 1% to 25%. Third, everything is causal, so a live device could do the same. Whether this improves accuracy is the question for the results, next.
